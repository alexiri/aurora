# FIX-288: an approval raised further down a delegation chain reaches the person who asked (FIX-252's open work, 2026-11-28, security)

## Why

D-489 (owner 2026-11-26: approvals "need to travel back to the original request point") was built by FIX-252 for
**one hop**. When a person asks agent A, A delegates to B, and B needs an approval, the card goes to the person's
conversation.

FIX-252's PROGRESS row says: "approvals raised inside a delegated conversation by an onward call are NOT covered yet:
open work". The case is A → B → C, where B, working on A's task, delegates on to C and C needs an approval. C's card is
delivered to the origin of C's task, which is B's task conversation. No person reads that conversation. So the person
who started the chain never learns that something waits for a decision, and the task waits until it expires.

## Decision (Orchestrator, within D-489)

*(Corrected 2026-11-28, D-509: the first version walked to the chain's first conversation whoever had asked, which is
wrong when the requester changes on the way. It also assumed a delegation depth limit that did not exist.)*

Only a person speaking in a task conversation can open a further task there, and that person need not be the one the
chain started with. An approval raised in a task travels up the task origins **while the requester stays the same
person**: walk the task's origin, and the origin's own task origin, until a conversation that isn't a peer task
conversation, or until the task conversation where somebody else asked.
- Everything D-489 says applies there: the D-178 redaction, the decide buttons only for someone with `can_approve` in
  the **target** workspace (C's), otherwise Ask an approver / Cancel, the outcome posted back to that conversation, and
  each delegating agent in the chain getting its follow-up turn in its own conversation.
- A chain is at most `PEER_CHAIN_DEPTH_MAX` = 8 tasks deep (new): the message that would open a ninth is refused in
  plain words. The same limit bounds the walk, which never crosses into a conversation the chain didn't come from.
- A live onward task is a wake registration of the delegating task; its report, its messages and an outcome further
  down wake it through the one wake helper. While the delegating agent is frozen only its own wake-up is held, and the
  sweep delivers it once after the unfreeze.
- If the owner wants the card in every intermediate conversation too, that's a later change; record it as an open
  question.

## Change

- `peer_tasks` (approval delivery): resolve the human origin through the chain, under the lock order written at the
  top of `peer_tasks/service.rs`.
- The approval notice and the outcome notice use that origin.
- The requester whose rights decide the buttons is the person in that origin conversation, the chain's first
  requester. Being the requester grants no right; `can_approve` in C's workspace decides.
- An approval decided anywhere ends the wait for every task in the chain that waits on it, exactly once.
- Update `docs/07` and `docs/10`, and amend C-1167 if its shape changes.

## Tests (each must fail before the fix)

- A → B → C with an approval at C: the card is in the person's conversation, with the right buttons for a person with
  and without `can_approve` in C's workspace.
- Approving posts the outcome there, and B and A each get their follow-up turn.
- Denying or expiring: the same.
- A chain at the depth limit still delivers.
- A delegated conversation whose chain has no human origin (an automation started it): the card goes where FIX-252
  sends it today, the automation's own conversation. Say what that is.
- Delivery twice (bus and sweep) is a single card.

## Completion report

### Lane `alexiri/fix-288` (2026-11-28)

#### What the code showed, and the four answers (coordinator, 2026-11-28: all recommendations)

1. A non-person turn cannot open a task, so A → B → C exists only through a person's turn in B's task conversation.
   Nothing was widened. That person can differ from the first requester: the walk stops where the requester changes
   (D-509(a)).
2. There was no delegation depth limit: `PEER_CHAIN_DEPTH_MAX` = 8 (D-509(b)).
3. B had no wake registered for C, so B's follow-up turn after a decision would have cancelled B's task while C still
   worked: a live onward task now holds the delegating task.
4. A frozen B holds only its own wake-up.

**An automation chain.** An automation's own turn is spoken by the platform, so it cannot open a task. "A chain with
no human origin" therefore does not exist. A chain can *start* in an automation's thread, when a person writes there;
the card goes to that thread (the first conversation that is no task conversation), as FIX-252 does today.

#### What changed

- **Migration `20261201000293_peer_chains.sql`** (renumber at merge if taken): the SQL function
  `peer_task_card_home(task, max_depth)`, the one statement of the walk, used by the service, the card fan-out and the
  three sweep queries; the table `peer_held_wakes`.
- `peer_tasks/service.rs`: the waiting, outcome and settling notices go to the card's home; D-178 follows that
  conversation's link; `follow_up` is the one path that tells a delegating agent something (report, close line,
  outcome), and in a live task's conversation it is a wake through the wake helper; a message from the onward task
  wakes a waiting requester; the depth refusal (before any approval, and again when an approved message opens);
  held wake-ups and their sweep pass; the locks below.
- **Settling the card.** A task closed with its approval pending reports to its own origin, which is not where the
  card stands. The close line is now posted at the card's home too, so the web card folds.
- `cards/fanout.rs`, `repo/channel_cards.rs`: the channel card and its candidate query use the home.
- **No web change.** The FIX-252 card reads the notice's own fields and needs no chain names. No route, DTO, protocol
  type, audit action or UI text changed, so no OpenAPI or schema regeneration and no web drift guards.
- **Exactly once.** The outcome notice at the home marks the delivery. The approval task's wake, the notice and every
  follow-up are in its transaction. A frozen agent's wake-up is a row in `peer_held_wakes`, in the same transaction,
  taken under the task's locks.

#### Lock order of every new path

The order is written at the top of `peer_tasks/service.rs`: **task conversations (newest first, every one above) →
chain links (in id order) → task → other conversations.**

| Path | Locks, in order |
|---|---|
| Close (`close_task`: complete, both cancels, card cancel, expiry, unlink, budget) | task origins (now all levels, also above the pair's queued tasks) → chain links → task → notice conversations; the settling notice at the home is a task origin already held or an "other" one |
| Wake of a delegating task (`follow_up` → `wake_task` → `lock_for_task_conversation`) | its conversation (held by the close or delivery below) → its task origins (held) → its chain links (held) → its task row (new, below a link already held) |
| Delivery, outcome | approval conversation → task origins → (if C is woken) chain links, C's task → chain links when a delegating task sits above → the home notice → each delegator's wake as above |
| Delivery, raised | approval conversation → task origins → the home conversation |
| Turn end going `waiting` | conversation → task origins → task row (no link) |
| Reply from a task conversation, and the wake it may give the requester | conversation → task origins → chain links → pair link → task; then the wake as above |
| `message_agent` approval from a task conversation | that conversation → its task origins → link |
| Unlink | `link_origins` (now all levels) → link → closes as above |
| Held wake-ups (`deliver_held_wakes`) | conversation → task origins → chain links → task → the held rows → the turns |

#### Security notes

- **No new right.** Approve and Deny are still `can_approve` at the approval's workspace and its visibility, checked
  at decide time. Ask an approver and Cancel the task are still the stored requester of the approval's task; no route
  or action check changed. The walk only continues through the same requester, so that is also the person at the home.
- **No new audience.** The card never passes a change of requester, a deeper pre-limit chain falls back to the direct
  origin, and the walk follows only `origin_conversation_id`. D-178 uses the home's thread link, for the card and for
  the approver's name.
- **Wakes widen nothing.** Every wake of a delegating task goes through `wake_task`: requester re-checked, freeze
  honoured, charged to the message budget and the wake cap; the turn is the platform's, with no person.
- **Newly bounded or newly held.** Waiting on onward tasks counts toward the waiting caps (a task over one is
  cancelled, as before). A delegating task is now woken (and charged) for what its onward tasks send, also while it
  runs. Someone else who types in B's task conversation can hold the first person's task waiting, until its expiry.
- **Held text.** `peer_held_wakes.text` can hold a fenced, sanitized report, as `peer_tasks.report` does; rows go on
  delivery or with the task. A task that ends while frozen drops its rows at the next sweep.
- **Residual lock cases** (Postgres would abort one side with 40P01; each close or delivery is one transaction, and a
  delivery runs in a savepoint, so a decision is never lost): a path already holding one link (a reply, an approved
  message, unlinking, a promotion) then taking chain links, out of id order; the earlier ones in FIX-257's notes.
- **For FIX-284 (merge order).** Its `lock_for_decision` must lock, before the approval row, what the delivery takes:
  the conversation, `lock_task_origins` (all levels) and `lock_chain_links`. `lock_for_task_conversation` does exactly
  that, so it should call it rather than walk again. This lane did not change the approval-row ordering. A close now
  holds more conversations when it reaches `abandon_pending_of_conversation`, so the same fix matters there.
- **Upgrade.** A chain that was already waiting at deploy, with a decision delivered by the old code to the direct
  origin and a home further up, can get that notice and follow-up once more at the home. Old replicas during a rolling
  update use the direct origin.
